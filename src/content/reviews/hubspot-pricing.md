HubSpot pricing looks simple until you try to calculate an actual first-year bill. The edition is only the starting point. Your final cost can also depend on billing term, marketing contacts, Core Seats, onboarding, HubSpot Credits, and optional add-ons.

This guide was checked against HubSpot's public Marketing Hub pricing page, HubSpot's official pricing guide, and its marketing-contact documentation on July 29, 2026. That matters because the live page can show a temporary Starter promotion while the monthly configurator and standard rate show a different number.

## HubSpot pricing at a glance

| Plan | Advertised base price | Included marketing contacts | Included Core Seats | Required onboarding |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Free tools | $0 | No paid contact tier | Up to 2 users in the official pricing guide | $0 |
| Marketing Hub Starter | Promotional annual price may start at $7/seat; standard monthly configuration around $20 | 1,000 | Priced per seat | $0 |
| Marketing Hub Professional | $800/mo billed annually or $890/mo billed monthly | 2,000 | 3 | $3,000 one time |
| Marketing Hub Enterprise | $3,600/mo | 10,000 | 5 | $7,000 one time |

The important number is not the $0 entry point. It is the gap between Starter and Professional. A business can move from roughly $20 per month to at least $12,600 in first-year Professional costs when paying annually: $9,600 subscription plus $3,000 onboarding.

That is why HubSpot is either excellent value or severe overkill. There is not much middle ground.

## Why two Starter prices appear

At the time of our July 29 check, HubSpot's live Marketing Hub page displayed a limited-time annual Starter price starting at $7 per seat, while HubSpot's bundle configurator showed a standard $20 monthly Marketing Hub Starter configuration. HubSpot also states that displayed offers can be limited to new customers and can expire.

Use the promotional number only if the checkout page still shows it for your account, country, billing term, and seat count. For planning, use the standard monthly configuration and treat any promotion as a temporary discount rather than the permanent cost.

## Free tools: useful for validation, not a full marketing system

HubSpot's free tools are a sensible way to test the contact record, forms, basic email marketing, live chat, and CRM workflow before paying. The official pricing guide lists up to two users and 2,000 marketing email sends per month for the free package.

The practical limits are just as important as the $0 price:

- HubSpot branding remains on free marketing assets.
- Automation is basic.
- Reporting is limited.
- The email allowance is too small for an active list at scale.
- Free access does not mean every HubSpot product or premium feature is free.

Free is best for a founder validating a sales process, a small consultancy centralizing contacts, or a team preparing a migration. It is not a substitute for Professional workflows and attribution.

## Marketing Hub Starter: the low-cost bridge

Starter includes 1,000 marketing contacts and is priced per seat. It is the right tier when you mainly need branded forms and emails, simple automation, lead capture, and a cleaner version of the free tools.

Starter makes sense for:

- Small B2B teams with a straightforward sales process.
- Companies sending newsletters and basic nurture emails.
- Teams that want HubSpot CRM context without advanced campaign reporting.
- Businesses testing HubSpot before a larger migration.

Starter does not give you the full HubSpot experience people usually mean when they praise Marketing Hub. Advanced automation, deeper reporting, campaign orchestration, and governance are Professional or Enterprise territory.

The dangerous assumption is: "We can start at $20 and gradually add features." In practice, the next meaningful feature step is often an edition jump measured in hundreds of dollars per month.

## Marketing Hub Professional: where HubSpot becomes a platform

Professional is $800 per month when billed annually or $890 per month on the standard monthly rate. It includes 2,000 marketing contacts, three Core Seats, and 3,000 HubSpot Credits. HubSpot's official guide also lists a required $3,000 Professional onboarding fee.

Professional is where the platform starts to justify consolidation. It adds the automation, reporting, campaign management, personalization, and team features needed by a serious demand-generation operation.

### Real first-year Professional cost

| Cost item | Annual billing | Monthly billing |
|---|---:|---:|
| Base subscription | $9,600 | $10,680 |
| Required onboarding | $3,000 | $3,000 |
| First-year minimum | $12,600 | $13,680 |

This excludes extra marketing contacts, extra Core Seats, additional credits, paid integrations, migration labor, and partner consulting.

Annual billing saves $1,080 on the base subscription. That saving does not erase the onboarding fee, but it is large enough that a company committed to a full year should compare contract terms carefully.

## Marketing Hub Enterprise: governance at enterprise scale

Enterprise starts at $3,600 per month, includes 10,000 marketing contacts, five Core Seats, and 5,000 HubSpot Credits. HubSpot's official guide lists a required $7,000 Enterprise onboarding fee.

The base first-year cost is therefore at least $50,200: $43,200 for 12 months plus $7,000 onboarding. Additional contacts, seats, credits, migration, and consulting increase that figure.

Enterprise is designed for organizations that need governance rather than simply "more email." The value is in features such as advanced permissions, customer-journey analytics, multi-touch revenue attribution, approval processes, custom objects, and larger-team control.

A company should not buy Enterprise because its contact list is growing. It should buy Enterprise because operational complexity, attribution requirements, and team governance have become expensive problems.

## Marketing contacts: the pricing lever most teams miss

HubSpot distinguishes marketing contacts from non-marketing contacts. Only contacts you actively market to count toward the paid marketing-contact tier. Contacts retained for CRM history, service records, or sales reference can be marked non-marketing and do not count toward that tier.

This classification can materially reduce the bill. A database with 50,000 total CRM records does not automatically require a 50,000 marketing-contact plan if only 8,000 people receive marketing campaigns.

There are two catches:

1. Your selected contact tier sets the monthly marketing limit.
2. Moving into higher Professional tiers can happen in blocks. HubSpot's official pricing guide notes that passing 2,000 Professional marketing contacts can move an account to a 5,000-contact tier, adding roughly $250 per month.

Before importing a database, separate active prospects from customers, unsubscribed contacts, bounced contacts, vendors, employees, and historical records. Poor classification turns free CRM storage into an unnecessary marketing bill.

## Seats, credits, and add-ons

Paid Marketing Hub plans also have a seat model. Starter is priced per seat. Professional includes three Core Seats, with additional Core Seats starting around $45 per month on annual billing. Enterprise includes five Core Seats, with additional seats starting around $75 per month.

View-only access can be free, so not every stakeholder needs a paid Core Seat. Reserve paid seats for people who build campaigns, edit assets, configure workflows, or administer the system.

HubSpot Credits are another variable. The live pricing page listed 500 credits for Starter, 3,000 for Professional, and 5,000 for Enterprise. Credits power selected AI and usage-based features. Heavy use can create another cost layer beyond seats and contacts.

The total-cost formula is therefore:

**Edition + billing term + marketing-contact tier + Core Seats + onboarding + credits/add-ons + migration labor.**

## The Starter-to-Professional cliff

The central pricing decision is not Free versus Starter. It is Starter versus Professional.

| Scenario | Likely fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Founder testing CRM and forms | Free tools | Validate the workflow before paying |
| Small team sending basic campaigns | Starter | Low entry cost and 1,000 marketing contacts |
| B2B team needing serious automation and reporting | Professional | The full demand-generation platform begins here |
| Large organization needing governance and attribution | Enterprise | Permissions, orchestration, and advanced analytics justify the premium |

If you only need newsletters, forms, and simple welcome emails, Professional is usually poor value. [MailerLite pricing](/reviews/mailerlite-pricing/) and [Brevo pricing](/reviews/brevo-pricing/) are dramatically lower.

If you need complex automation but can use a separate CRM, [ActiveCampaign pricing](/reviews/activecampaign-pricing/) is the strongest benchmark. ActiveCampaign can deliver deeper workflow logic at a fraction of HubSpot Professional's base price, while HubSpot wins on the unified customer record and CRM experience.

## Who should pay for Professional

Professional is easier to justify when all of these are true:

- Marketing and sales share a pipeline and need one customer record.
- The team will actively use workflows, campaign reporting, personalization, and attribution.
- Revenue per customer is high enough that one or two additional wins can cover $12,600 or more in first-year cost.
- At least several employees will use HubSpot every week.
- The company has clean contact governance and can control the marketing-contact tier.

It is difficult to justify when the business sends a monthly newsletter, has no sales team, does not use attribution, or cannot name the manual work the platform will replace.

## A practical buying checklist

Before signing a contract, request a written quote that states:

1. Billing term and renewal price.
2. Included marketing contacts and the price of the next contact tier.
3. Included Core Seats and each additional seat price.
4. Required onboarding fee.
5. Included HubSpot Credits and overage rules.
6. Any temporary discount and its expiration date.
7. Migration, integration, and partner-consulting costs.
8. The process and timing for marking contacts non-marketing.

Model the bill at today's database size and at the size you expect in 12 months. The second number is more useful than the promotional checkout price.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does HubSpot Marketing Hub cost in 2026?

Free tools cost $0. Starter can show a limited-time annual promotion and has a standard monthly configuration around $20. Professional is $800 per month annually or $890 monthly, plus $3,000 onboarding. Enterprise starts at $3,600 per month plus $7,000 onboarding.

### Is HubSpot really free?

HubSpot offers genuinely useful free CRM and marketing tools, but free does not include the advanced automation, reporting, governance, or volume of Professional and Enterprise. HubSpot branding and usage limits also apply.

### What is the real first-year cost of HubSpot Professional?

The minimum is about $12,600 with annual billing: $9,600 for the subscription plus $3,000 onboarding. Standard monthly billing totals about $13,680 in year one before extra contacts, seats, credits, or services.

### Does HubSpot charge for every CRM contact?

No. Only marketing contacts count toward the Marketing Hub contact tier. Non-marketing contacts can remain in the CRM without counting toward that paid limit, provided they are classified correctly.

### How many contacts are included?

Starter includes 1,000 marketing contacts, Professional includes 2,000, and Enterprise includes 10,000 according to HubSpot's official pricing materials checked in July 2026.

### How many users are included?

Starter is priced per seat. Professional includes three Core Seats and Enterprise includes five. View-only seats can be free, while additional Core Seats cost extra.

### Is HubSpot Professional worth $800-$890 per month?

It can be for a B2B team that replaces multiple tools and uses the CRM, workflows, reporting, and attribution every week. It is usually not worth it for simple newsletters or basic automation.

### Why does HubSpot Starter show different prices?

HubSpot can display limited-time new-customer promotions on annual plans while its standard monthly configurator shows a higher amount. Confirm the final checkout price, contract term, and renewal price in writing.

## Final verdict

HubSpot's free tools and Starter plan are easy to recommend for testing and simple B2B workflows. Professional is a different purchase: it is a minimum five-figure first-year platform commitment, not a normal email marketing upgrade.

Buy Professional when one shared CRM and marketing system will replace enough tools, reporting work, and sales friction to recover the cost. Otherwise, keep HubSpot CRM free and pair it with a lower-cost email platform.
