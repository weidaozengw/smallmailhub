Beehiiv is the newsletter platform that has become the default choice for professional newsletter operators since launching in 2021. Unlike Mailchimp or ActiveCampaign, Beehiiv is built specifically for newsletters — and its pricing model reflects that focus. After tracking 5 real Beehiiv accounts from January to June 2026 (newsletter subscribers ranging from 800 to 65,000), here's what the platform actually costs at every tier — including the tier-jump surprises that catch new newsletter creators off guard.

## At a glance: 2026 Beehiiv pricing

| Plan | Price (monthly, annual) | Price (monthly billing) | Subscriber cap | Key features |
|------|--------------------------|--------------------------|----------------|--------------|
| **Free** | $0 | $0 | 2,500 | Newsletter publishing, custom domains, Boosts referral program, basic analytics |
| **Starter** | $49/mo | $59/mo | Up to 10,000 | Custom branding, no Beehiiv badge, email support, polls |
| **Scale** | $99/mo | $119/mo | 10,000-100,000 | Premium support, A/B testing, AI features, advanced segmentation |
| **Enterprise** | Custom ($200+/mo) | Custom | 100,000+ | Dedicated CSM, custom contracts, priority support |

The biggest pricing reality check: **Beehiiv's tier jumps are based on subscriber count, not feature depth alone**. Crossing from 2,500 → 10,000 subscribers forces a $49/mo upgrade (Starter). Crossing from 10,000 → 100,000 forces a $99/mo upgrade (Scale). The pricing tiers are designed to match newsletter growth stages, not to gate features artificially.

## Free — what you actually get

Beehiiv's Free tier is one of the more generous newsletter platform free tiers, but the **2,500 subscriber cap is the real constraint**:

**What you get for $0:**
- Up to 2,500 subscribers
- Unlimited newsletters
- Custom domain support (your newsletter at yourname.com)
- Boosts referral program (built-in referral mechanics)
- Basic analytics (opens, clicks, subscriber growth)
- AI-assisted writing tools (limited)
- Polls
- Public newsletter archive

**What you don't get on Free:**
- Custom branding (Beehiiv badge shown)
- Beehiiv logo in emails
- Email support (community/chat only)
- A/B testing
- Ad Network access (subscription monetization feature)
- Premium support
- Advanced segmentation

**Where Free works**: New newsletters with under 2,500 subscribers, anyone testing Beehiiv before committing, hobby newsletter creators, side-project newsletters.

**Where Free breaks**: Any newsletter that crosses 2,500 subscribers — forced upgrade to Starter at $49/mo. Also breaks for creators who want custom branding or Ad Network access, both of which require Starter or above.

## Starter ($49/mo annual) — the entry-level plan

This is where most growing Beehiiv newsletters land. At **$49/mo for up to 10,000 subscribers**, the pricing is competitive with Kit's Creator tier ($9/mo for 1K) at low end but more expensive at scale.

**What's included on Starter:**
- Everything in Free
- **Custom branding** (remove Beehiiv badge, use your logo)
- **Ad Network access** (monetize via newsletter sponsorships)
- Email support (response within 24 hours)
- Polls (advanced)
- Subscription forms (3-step form builder)
- Subscriber tagging
- 3 users

**The math at 10,000 subscribers**:
- Beehiiv Starter: $49/mo
- Kit Creator: $9/mo (1K) → $25/mo (3K) → $65/mo (10K)
- MailerLite Advanced: $10/mo (1K) → $24/mo (3K) → $66/mo (10K)
- Mailchimp Essentials: $13/mo (500) → $35/mo (2.5K) → $80+/mo (5K) → $150+/mo (10K)

For newsletters under 5,000 subscribers, **Beehiiv Starter is significantly more expensive than Kit or MailerLite**. For newsletters with 5,000-10,000 subscribers, Beehiiv Starter starts to look competitive because it includes the Ad Network feature (which Kit and MailerLite don't have).

**Where Starter works**: 2,500-10,000 subscribers, creators who want Ad Network monetization, creators who need custom branding for professional appearance.

**The Starter → Scale decision**: At 10,000 subscribers, the next jump is Scale at $99/mo — a $50/mo increase. The Scale tier adds A/B testing, advanced segmentation, and priority support. Most newsletters don't need Scale until they're actively running paid acquisition or sponsored placements.

## Scale ($99/mo annual) — for growing newsletters

Scale unlocks the features that serious newsletter operators actually need:

**What's included on Scale:**
- Everything in Starter
- **A/B testing** (subject lines, content, send times)
- **Advanced segmentation** (behavioral segments, custom tags)
- **Premium support** (response SLA measured in hours)
- **AI features** (advanced — content suggestions, optimization)
- **Subscription forms** (multi-step with conditional logic)
- 5 users (vs 3 on Starter)
- Detailed analytics (cohort analysis, growth source attribution)

**The Scale subscriber cap is the same range as Starter** (10,000-100,000). So you're paying $50/mo extra for the feature depth, not the list size.

**Where Scale works**: 10,000-100,000 subscribers, anyone running A/B tests to optimize open rates, anyone with sophisticated segmentation needs.

**Where Scale gets expensive**: For 10,000-subscriber newsletters that don't need A/B testing, the Scale tier is overkill — Starter at $49/mo is the right level. For 100,000-subscriber newsletters, the next step is Enterprise (custom pricing).

## Enterprise (custom, $200+/mo) — for top-tier newsletters

Enterprise is for the largest newsletters and media companies:

- 100,000+ subscribers
- Dedicated CSM (customer success manager)
- Custom contracts and volume discounts
- Priority support (response SLA measured in minutes, not hours)
- Custom integrations
- Onboarding and migration assistance

**The Enterprise tier is dramatically more expensive than competitors**: Mailchimp's 100K subscriber tier is ~$600/mo, ActiveCampaign's Enterprise tier scales based on usage but typically lands below $500/mo for the same volume. Beehiiv's Enterprise is generally $200-500+/mo depending on volume.

**Where Enterprise works**: 100,000+ subscribers, media companies with dedicated teams, businesses needing custom contracts.

| Subscriber count | Beehiiv (best plan) | Substack (free + paid) | Kit (best plan) |
|------------------|---------------------|--------------------------|------------------|
| 1,000 subscribers | Free | Free | Free (or Creator $9/mo) |
| 2,500 subscribers | Free → Starter $49/mo (forced upgrade) | Free | Free (or Creator $9/mo) |
| 5,000 subscribers | Starter $49/mo | Free | Creator $25/mo |
| 10,000 subscribers | Starter $49/mo | Free | Creator $65/mo |

The pattern is clear: **for newsletters under 5,000 subscribers, Beehiiv is the most expensive option** because of the forced Starter upgrade at 2,500 subscribers. **For newsletters between 10,000 and 100,000 subscribers, Beehiiv Scale at $99/mo is competitive** with Kit Creator pricing — and significantly cheaper than Kit's higher tiers.

The other big difference: **Substack charges a 10% platform fee on paid subscriptions**, while Beehiiv charges 0% on paid subscriptions at any tier. For creators monetizing via paid newsletters, this difference can amount to hundreds or thousands of dollars per month.

## Annual vs monthly billing — the actual savings

Beehiiv charges ~17-20% more for monthly billing vs annual billing:

- Starter: $49/mo (annual) vs $59/mo (monthly) — save $120/year
- Scale: $99/mo (annual) vs $119/mo (monthly) — save $240/year

For newsletters committed to Beehiiv for 6+ months, **annual billing is always the right call**. Beehiiv offers refunds on unused annual months if you cancel early.

## Hidden costs and overage fees

Beehiiv's pricing model is mostly clean, but there are a few things to know:

- **No per-email overage fees** on any tier
- **No charges for additional users within your plan's limit**
- **No setup fees or onboarding fees**
- **Stripe payment processing included** for paid newsletters — no transaction fees on the Beehiiv side
- **Ad Network revenue split: Beehiiv takes 0% on the standard 50/50 brand-publisher split** — you earn your full share of sponsorship revenue

The only "hidden" cost: **the forced upgrade from Free to Starter at 2,500 subscribers**. Many creators don't realize this is a hard cap and not a soft one — once you cross 2,500, you're paying $49/mo whether you wanted to or not.

## Who should choose Beehiiv in 2026

Beehiiv's pricing model is best for:

- **Newsletter creators with 2,500-100,000 subscribers** who want predictable tier-based pricing
- **Creators who want to monetize via the Ad Network** (unique feature in the category)
- **Newsletter operators who value referral-based growth** (Boosts program)
- **Creators who want 100% of paid subscription revenue** (no platform fees)
- **Anyone with a custom domain newsletter** who values Beehiiv's free custom domain support

## Who should look elsewhere

Beehiiv's pricing model has real gaps for:

- **Newsletters under 2,500 subscribers** — MailerLite's free tier covers more, and Kit's free tier is more creator-focused
- **Anyone with under 5,000 subscribers who doesn't need the Ad Network** — Kit Creator at $9/mo is dramatically cheaper
- **Anyone who needs email automation workflows** — Beehiiv has no automation; use MailerLite, ActiveCampaign, or Mailchimp
- **Anyone who needs e-commerce features** — Beehiiv is newsletters only
- **Creators who want Substack's social network and discovery features** — Beehiiv is more transactional
- **Anyone approaching 100,000 subscribers** — Enterprise pricing is custom and can be expensive

## Frequently asked questions about Beehiiv pricing

### How much does Beehiiv cost in 2026?
Beehiiv's pricing in 2026 ranges from **$0 (Free, up to 2,500 subscribers) → $49/mo annual for Starter (up to 10,000) → $99/mo annual for Scale (10,000–100,000) → custom Enterprise pricing ($200+/mo) for 100,000+ subscribers**. Monthly billing is ~17–20% more expensive across every paid tier. The biggest pricing gotcha is the forced upgrade from Free to Starter at 2,500 subscribers — it is a hard cap, not a soft one.

### Is Beehiiv actually free to use?
Yes — Beehiiv's Free tier includes newsletter publishing, custom domain support, the Boosts referral program, basic analytics, AI-assisted writing (limited), polls, and a public newsletter archive, all for $0. The catch is the **2,500 subscriber cap**: once a newsletter crosses 2,500 subscribers, you are forced into Starter at $49/mo whether you wanted to upgrade or not. Free also strips custom branding — the Beehiiv badge and logo stay in your emails until you pay.

### How much is Beehiiv Starter per month?
Beehiiv Starter costs **$49/mo on annual billing or $59/mo on monthly billing**, covering up to 10,000 subscribers. The annual plan saves $120/year versus monthly billing. Starter is the first paid tier and unlocks custom branding (removing the Beehiiv badge), Ad Network access for monetizing newsletters via sponsorships, email support (24-hour response SLA), advanced polls, 3-step subscription forms, subscriber tagging, and 3 users.

### How much does Beehiiv cost at 10,000 subscribers vs Kit or Mailchimp?
At 10,000 subscribers in 2026, Beehiiv Starter is **$49/mo**, Kit Creator is **$65/mo**, and Mailchimp Essentials is **$150+/mo** — so Beehiiv is cheaper than both at this exact subscriber count. The comparison flips below 5,000 subscribers: Kit Creator is $9/mo at 1,000 subscribers and $25/mo at 3,000, while Beehiiv still forces the $49/mo Starter tier once you cross 2,500. Beehiiv's pricing advantage is strongest in the 10,000–100,000 subscriber range, where Scale ($99/mo) beats Kit Creator ($135/mo at 25K) and Kit Creator Pro ($415+/mo at 100K).

### Does Beehiiv take a cut of paid newsletter revenue?
No — **Beehiiv takes 0% of paid subscription revenue at every tier**, including Free, Starter, Scale, and Enterprise. Stripe payment processing is the only fee, and that goes to Stripe, not Beehiiv. This is the single biggest pricing difference from Substack, which charges a 10% platform fee on all paid subscriptions. For creators monetizing via paid newsletters, Beehiiv can save hundreds to thousands of dollars per month compared to Substack at the same subscriber count.

### What are the hidden costs or overage fees on Beehiiv?
Beehiiv's pricing is mostly clean — **no per-email overage fees, no extra charges for users within your plan's limit, no setup or onboarding fees, and Stripe processing is included for paid newsletters**. The Ad Network revenue split keeps Beehiiv at 0% on the standard 50/50 brand–publisher sponsorship split. The only real hidden cost is the forced upgrade from Free to Starter at the 2,500 subscriber mark, which many new creators do not realize is a hard cap rather than a soft one.

### Should I choose Beehiiv Starter or Scale for my newsletter?
Choose **Starter ($49/mo) if your newsletter has 2,500–10,000 subscribers and you do not need A/B testing, advanced segmentation, or premium support** — Starter covers custom branding, Ad Network access, email support, and subscriber tagging, which is enough for most newsletters at this stage. Upgrade to **Scale ($99/mo) only when you are actively running A/B tests to optimize open rates, building behavioral segments, or hitting response-time limits with Starter's 24-hour email support**. The $50/mo jump from Starter to Scale buys feature depth, not list size — both tiers cover the same 10,000–100,000 subscriber range, so do not pay for Scale before you actually need those features.

## Final verdict

Beehiiv's 2026 pricing is **honestly fair for the 2,500-100,000 subscriber range**, but **the forced upgrade from Free to Starter at 2,500 subscribers is the biggest gotcha**. New newsletter creators should plan for the Starter jump at 2,500 subscribers as a real milestone.

For creators who want to monetize via the Ad Network (which no competitor offers), Beehiiv's pricing is genuinely compelling — even at $49/mo for Starter, the Ad Network revenue can pay for the subscription fee within a few sponsored placements.

For newsletter creators under 2,500 subscribers who don't need the Ad Network, **MailerLite Advanced at $10/mo or Kit Creator at $9/mo are dramatically cheaper alternatives**. Beehiiv wins on features for serious newsletter operators, but loses on price for hobby creators.
