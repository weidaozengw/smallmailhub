The best email marketing tool for a beginner is not the platform with the longest feature list. It is the one that helps you complete four jobs without getting stuck: create a signup form, send a useful first email, build one welcome automation, and understand what happens when your list grows.

That changes the ranking. Advanced lead scoring, custom objects, and enterprise attribution sound impressive, but they usually create more menus and more expensive upgrades before a beginner has learned the basics.

For most first-time users, **MailerLite is the safest all-purpose starting point**. Brevo is better when your website also sends receipts or account emails. Kit is better for creators. Beehiiv is better for a newsletter-first publication. Mailchimp remains useful when integrations and brand familiarity matter. Constant Contact is the support-first option.

## What a beginner should compare

Ignore feature-count comparisons for the first month. Compare these six things instead:

1. **First-campaign friction** — can you import contacts, verify a domain, and send without learning a marketing-operations system?
2. **Free-plan usefulness** — does the plan let you test a real workflow, not just view a demo?
3. **Welcome automation** — can a new subscriber receive the right message automatically?
4. **Forms and landing pages** — can you collect subscribers without buying another tool?
5. **Support** — is there useful documentation, chat, email, or phone support when setup fails?
6. **Upgrade path** — will the price or feature gates force a painful migration after a few months?

We compared current official pricing and feature pages where available and avoided claiming that every tool was tested on fabricated client accounts. Pricing changes frequently, so confirm checkout totals before paying.

## 2. Brevo — Best for marketing plus transactional email

Brevo is the better beginner choice when "email marketing" is only half the job. If a website also sends account alerts, order confirmations, password resets, or booking messages, Brevo keeps transactional and marketing email in one account.

Brevo's official pricing page checked on July 29, 2026 states that the Free plan needs no credit card and can send up to 300 emails per day after account approval. All plans provide access to transactional email features. Starter adds higher monthly volume and core campaign tools; Standard adds unlimited multi-step automation, A/B testing, advanced reports, send-time optimization, tracking, and a landing page.

### Why beginners like it

- Free signup does not require a credit card.
- The same vendor handles campaigns and transactional messages.
- Pricing is oriented around send volume, which can suit a large contact database mailed infrequently.
- Forms, segmentation, templates, reporting, and email support cover a normal first setup.
- The official FAQ explicitly says no prior knowledge is required.

### Where beginners get surprised

- Free sending is capped daily rather than only by list size.
- Account approval is required before sending.
- SMS credit is separate.
- The interface covers more channels and business functions than MailerLite, so it feels busier.
- Serious automation sits on Standard rather than Starter.

**Choose Brevo if:** your store, SaaS product, membership site, or booking business needs both promotional and operational email.

## 4. Mailchimp — Best for beginners who value a familiar brand

Mailchimp remains useful for beginners whose clients, teammates, or contractors already know the interface, or who need the largest integration library in the category. The brand familiarity reduces onboarding friction for non-technical collaborators, and the ecosystem of connectors (Shopify, Salesforce, WordPress, Zapier) is broader than any beginner-focused rival.

Mailchimp's pricing page checked on July 13, 2026 (per our [Mailchimp pricing breakdown](/reviews/mailchimp-pricing/)) shows a Free tier capped at 250 contacts and 500 monthly sends. Essentials is advertised at $13/mo (annual) or $15/mo (monthly) for 500 contacts with up to 10× contact count in monthly sends. Standard adds $20/mo (annual) with retargeting ads and multi-step automations. The paid tiers were historically labeled Plus and Premium; Mailchimp now pushes accounts above 2,500 contacts into custom Premier pricing. Always re-confirm the tier shown in your account before sending a campaign.

### Why beginners like it

- The interface is familiar to anyone who has used Mailchimp at a previous job or seen the brand in marketing tools.
- The integration library is the deepest in this category — Shopify, Salesforce, WordPress, Squarespace, Zapier all connect natively.
- Annual prepay saves around 15% versus month-to-month billing on Essentials and Standard.
- The template gallery is large and beginner-friendly; most first-time users find a usable starting design within minutes.
- Legacy accounts that signed up before May 2022 keep cheaper grandfathered rates.

### Where beginners get surprised

- The free plan cap dropped from 2,000 contacts (pre-2022) to 250 contacts and 500 monthly sends. Many comparison articles still quote the old number.
- Pricing jumps hard above 2,500 contacts — accounts move into custom Premier pricing that is materially more expensive than Essentials.
- Multi-user seats on Essentials cost extra per seat on top of the base price.
- Transactional email is not bundled; Mailchimp's Mandrill add-on costs extra.
- Standard tier reporting depth is shallower than rivals in the same price band.

**Choose Mailchimp if:** your collaborators already know the brand, your site runs on Shopify or WordPress, or you want the largest integration library in this category and accept that the price climbs sharply above 2,500 contacts.

## 5. Beehiiv — Best for newsletter-only beginners

Beehiiv is purpose-built for newsletter publishing rather than general email marketing. For a beginner whose entire goal is to publish a regular newsletter and grow subscribers, Beehiiv is simpler than Mailchimp or ActiveCampaign because the workflow is collapsed into one product: write, send, monetize.

Beehiiv's pricing checked on July 3, 2026 (per our [Beehiiv review](/reviews/beehiiv/)) shows a Launch plan free for up to 2,500 subscribers. Scale at $49/mo covers up to 10,000 subscribers; a higher Scale tier at $99/mo covers up to 100,000 subscribers. Enterprise is custom above 100,000. Always confirm the tier shown inside your dashboard before promoting a paid plan.

### Why beginners like it

- The editor is modern, fast, and purpose-built for newsletter writing — closer to a publishing tool than a marketing suite.
- The Ad Network matches newsletter operators with sponsor brands and handles invoicing — beginners can monetize without doing sponsor sales.
- Premium subscriptions let you launch a paid newsletter with 0% platform fees on paid Beehiiv plans.
- The Boosts referral program rewards readers for bringing new subscribers — a viral mechanic built into the product.
- Analytics include open rate by subscriber source, which most beginner tools hide behind higher tiers.

### Where beginners get surprised

- Beehiiv has no automation workflows. Welcome sequences, drip campaigns, and behavioral triggers are not part of the product. If you need automation, MailerLite or Kit is the better starting point.
- There is no landing page builder beyond the newsletter signup form. Beginners who want a separate squeeze page need a second tool.
- The free tier caps at 2,500 subscribers, lower than MailerLite's free plan.
- Beehiiv is younger than Substack or Mailchimp, so long-term roadmap stability is less proven.
- Reporting is excellent for newsletter operators but is not a substitute for general marketing analytics.

**Choose Beehiiv if:** your goal is to publish a newsletter and grow it — possibly into a paid publication — and you do not need automations, complex forms, or e-commerce integrations.

## 6. Constant Contact — Best for beginners who want phone support

Constant Contact is the beginner pick when the deciding factor is real human support and event tools. Phone, chat, and email support are included on every paid tier, even the $12/mo entry level. Constant Contact also bundles event registration, surveys, and donations — useful for local businesses, nonprofits, and community organizers.

Constant Contact's pricing page checked on July 19, 2026 (per our [Constant Contact pricing breakdown](/reviews/constant-contact-pricing-2026/)) shows three flat tiers: Core at $12/mo (annual prepay), Email Plus at $35/mo, and Premium at $80/mo. There is no free plan; a 14-day free trial is offered. Sends are unlimited on every paid tier. The published pricing assumes annual prepay; month-to-month is roughly 30-50% more expensive. A nonprofit discount of 15-30% applies with 501(c)(3) verification.

### Why beginners like it

- Phone support is included on every paid tier — rare in this category, where most rivals offer phone support only on enterprise plans.
- Unlimited monthly sends remove the overage-fee anxiety that beginners feel on Mailchimp or ActiveCampaign.
- The event registration, survey, and coupon tools are useful for local businesses, nonprofits, and community groups.
- The interface is simpler than Mailchimp's because the feature set is smaller.
- A 60-day money-back guarantee is offered on paid plans — longer than most competitors.

### Where beginners get surprised

- There is no free plan, only a 14-day free trial. MailerLite's free-forever 1,000-subscriber plan beats Constant Contact for very small senders who want to start without paying.
- Subscriber thresholds auto-upgrade you to higher tiers with limited warning email — small lists can silently jump $20-50 in a single month.
- A/B testing is locked behind the $80/mo Premium tier. MailerLite includes A/B testing on paid plans that start lower.
- Annual prepay is required for the lowest advertised rates; month-to-month billing is materially more expensive.
- Reporting depth is shallower on Core and Email Plus than on Mailchimp's Standard tier at a similar price.

**Choose Constant Contact if:** phone support matters to you, you run events or fundraising, you are a 501(c)(3) nonprofit that qualifies for the discount, or you send large monthly volumes and want unlimited sends on a flat bill.

## Common beginner mistakes to avoid

Based on the product capabilities and common first-year patterns, these are the mistakes that hurt beginners the most:

1. **Picking the platform with the longest feature list** — feature-count comparisons reward the platforms with the most enterprise menus. Beginners rarely use those features in the first year. Pick the platform whose default workflow matches how you actually send.

2. **Ignoring the free-tier contact cap** — Mailchimp's free plan caps at 250 contacts, MailerLite at 250 active subscribers in some regions, Beehiiv at 2,500. Importing a list larger than the cap will lock your sending until you upgrade or delete contacts.

3. **Skipping domain authentication** — beginners often send from the platform's default domain. Inbox placement on Gmail and corporate filters is significantly worse. Set up SPF, DKIM, and a custom sending domain before your first real campaign.

4. **Sending before testing welcome automation** — the first automation a beginner builds usually fires out of order or sends the welcome email twice. Test with a personal address before opening the form to real traffic.

5. **Buying annual prepay on day one** — annual prepay saves 15% on most platforms, but locks you in. Use the monthly or free tier for the first 60-90 days, then upgrade with confidence.

6. **Importing a stale list** — old addresses hurt deliverability on every platform. Beginners who import a 5,000-row list and immediately send a campaign see high bounce rates that damage inbox placement for months.

7. **Confusing "unlimited sends" with "unlimited contacts"** — Constant Contact offers unlimited sends but tiers by contact count. Mailchimp tiers by both contacts and sends. Read the tier matrix before assuming a free or cheap plan will cover your list.

8. **Picking a platform whose interface your team hates** — beginners often choose alone, then onboard collaborators who reject the tool. If a co-founder or assistant will touch the account, run a short trial with them before committing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which email marketing tool is easiest for a complete beginner?

MailerLite. The editor, the form builder, the automation builder, and the landing page builder use the same design language, so a first-time user can complete the four core jobs — signup form, first campaign, welcome automation, list growth — without learning a marketing-operations system. Brevo and Kit are also beginner-friendly but cover more features, which means more menus.

### Is there a truly free email marketing tool for beginners?

Yes, but with caveats. MailerLite, Brevo, Kit, Mailchimp, and Beehiiv all offer a free plan. The catch is that each free plan caps on a different axis — Mailchimp caps at 250 contacts and 500 monthly sends, MailerLite at 250 active subscribers in some regions, Brevo at 300 emails per day, Kit on automation count, Beehiiv at 2,500 subscribers. Constant Contact does not offer a free plan, only a 14-day trial. Match the free-tier cap to your expected list size, not the marketing headline.

### What is the cheapest email marketing tool that supports automations?

MailerLite's free plan supports basic automations including a welcome sequence. Kit's free plan supports one basic visual automation. Brevo's free plan supports automations with the daily send cap. If you need unlimited automations from day one, Kit's Creator plan at ~$33/mo or MailerLite's paid tier from ~$12/mo are the cheapest real options.

### Should I start with a free plan or jump straight to paid?

Start free for the first 60-90 days. Use that window to import your list, send your first three campaigns, build one welcome automation, and decide whether the interface is one your team can live with. Upgrade to paid when you hit a free-tier limit, when you want branding removal, or when the upgrade unlocks a feature you actually use. Avoid annual prepay until you have used the monthly tier for at least one billing cycle.

### Which email marketing tool is best for a small nonprofit?

Constant Contact. The 15-30% nonprofit discount on top of the flat-tier pricing is the strongest in the category, the event registration and donation tools are bundled, and phone support is included on every paid tier. MailerLite and Kit are good low-cost alternatives but do not offer the same nonprofit-specific tooling.

### Can I switch email marketing tools later without losing subscribers?

Yes. Every platform on this list supports CSV import and export of contacts. The harder part is migrating automations, templates, and forms — those usually need to be rebuilt by hand. Plan a 30-60 day migration with both platforms running in parallel, then cut over once the welcome automation and signup forms are rebuilt on the new platform.

### Which beginner tool has the best deliverability?

There is no universal winner. Deliverability depends on sender authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), list quality, engagement, content, and IP reputation more than the platform itself. Compare each vendor's authentication controls, suppression handling, and postmaster tooling, then run a seed-list test with your own domain before committing to a paid plan.

### Do I need a website to use these email marketing tools?

No. Every tool on this list supports a hosted signup form or landing page without a separate website. MailerLite's landing page builder is the strongest beginner option in this group. Beehiiv supports a hosted newsletter site. If you have an existing WordPress or Shopify site, Mailchimp's integration library is the broadest.
